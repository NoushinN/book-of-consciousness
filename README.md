# Theories of Consciousness

## Neuroscientific, Philosophical, Computational, Embodied, and Consciousness-First Perspectives

This repository contains a `bookdown` project for an academic book exploring and critically comparing major theories of consciousness across neuroscience, philosophy, cognitive science, artificial intelligence, embodiment, phenomenology, and physics.

The project aims to provide a comprehensive interdisciplinary overview of consciousness studies by integrating scientific, philosophical, computational, clinical, and consciousness-first perspectives into a single structured resource.

---

## About the Book

Consciousness remains one of the most profound and unresolved questions in science and philosophy.

This book examines how different disciplines attempt to explain:

* Subjective experience
* Self-awareness
* Perception
* Cognition
* Qualia
* Agency
* Conscious access
* Neural integration
* Embodiment
* Altered states
* Artificial intelligence and machine consciousness
* The relationship between mind and matter

The book critically evaluates competing theories, discusses their empirical support, compares their explanatory strengths and limitations, and highlights unresolved scientific, philosophical, and metaphysical challenges.

Rather than presenting consciousness as a single problem with one simple solution, the book treats consciousness as a multi-level phenomenon involving neural mechanisms, cognitive processes, lived experience, embodiment, selfhood, and foundational questions about reality itself.

## Objectives

This project aims to:

* Provide an accessible interdisciplinary introduction to consciousness studies
* Compare major theories of consciousness across shared criteria
* Bridge neuroscience, philosophy, cognitive science, AI, and phenomenology
* Explain the distinction between conscious access and phenomenal consciousness
* Explore computational and AI perspectives on consciousness
* Examine altered states, anesthesia, and disorders of consciousness
* Discuss embodied, enactive, and consciousness-first approaches
* Evaluate which problems are solved, partially explained, or still unresolved
* Highlight ethical implications of artificial consciousness, animal consciousness, and impaired consciousness
* Support students, researchers, educators, and general readers interested in consciousness research

## Intended Audience

This book is intended for:

* Students in philosophy, neuroscience, psychology, AI, cognitive science, and consciousness studies
* Researchers interested in theories of consciousness
* Educators and interdisciplinary scholars
* Readers interested in the science and philosophy of mind
* Readers exploring the relationship between consciousness, artificial intelligence, embodiment, and fundamental reality

## Key Themes

The book emphasizes several recurring themes:

* Consciousness is multidimensional rather than a single all-or-nothing phenomenon.
* Different theories often explain different targets, such as access, reportability, phenomenology, integration, selfhood, embodiment, or metaphysical foundations.
* Neuroscientific progress has been substantial, especially in the study of attention, anesthesia, altered states, and disorders of consciousness.
* The hard problem and explanatory gap remain unresolved.
* Artificial intelligence raises urgent questions about the difference between intelligence and subjective experience.
* Embodied and enactive approaches challenge purely brain-centered models.
* Consciousness-first frameworks, including panpsychism and T-Consciousness, broaden the discussion beyond standard materialist explanations.
* Future progress will likely require multi-level integration rather than reliance on a single theory.
